There's a difference between looking and seeing. Looking is a reflex; seeing is a choice.

I've been thinking about this distinction a lot lately, especially as the competition for our eyes grows fiercer. Every app, every notification, every algorithmically tuned headline is optimized to make you look — but none of them are particularly interested in whether you *see* anything.

## The Cost of Distraction

Attention isn't just a resource. It's closer to identity. What you pay attention to shapes what you notice, what you remember, what you care about. To let others commandeer your attention wholesale is to let them shape the contours of your inner life.

That's not a dramatic claim — it's just how cognition works.

## Reclaiming It

The answer, I think, isn't digital abstinence. It's more like hygiene. Intentional choices about what gets your time, and why.

Some things that have helped me:

- Keeping my phone out of the bedroom
- Reading long-form before short-form in the morning
- Taking photos of things I want to remember, not to share them

None of this is revolutionary. But done consistently, it changes the texture of a day.
